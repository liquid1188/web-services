What a migration actually involves

Four things have to move: the content, the blog or catalog, the domain, and the search rankings. Most botched migrations lose one of them. Here is how each is handled.

  • Content. Every page is rebuilt by hand in a design made for your work, not copied into a template. Text, images, and downloads come over.
  • Blog, catalog, or gallery. Posts and products are migrated with a script, not retyped. Roxane Salonen's site moved with 2,300 posts intact.
  • Domain. Your domain points to the new site at the moment we launch. No downtime, and email keeps working because I check the mail records before touching anything.
  • Old links. Every old address is redirected to its new page, so links from other sites and search results still land where they should.

What you get instead

A hand-coded site. Hosting is free on GitHub Pages and there are no platform fees, so your only ongoing cost is the domain, about $10 a year. The files are yours, and if you ever want to move the site elsewhere I hand it over and help with the switch. Custom and Full Build sites include a self-serve editor so you can change text, swap photos, and post updates without touching code.

Wix and Squarespace, honestly

They are good for getting a first site up in an afternoon. They become a problem when the site matters: the monthly fee never ends, page speed is out of your control, and your content lives inside their editor. If you are past the first-site stage, moving is usually cheaper within two years than staying.

Who this is for
Authors, musicians, artists, parishes, and small nonprofits on Wix, Squarespace, WordPress, GoDaddy, or Weebly who want a site they own, that loads fast, and that does not bill them monthly.
