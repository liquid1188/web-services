Events, ticketing, and community

DC Social Collective

A website for a young-adult events company in Washington, DC, built to sell the next night and keep every night before it.

  • Moved from Squarespace
  • 43 photo albums
  • More than 6,000 photographs
  • Live ticketing
  • Self-serve uploads

The brief

DC Social Collective hosts nights out for young adults around Washington, DC, with dance lessons, live bands, and a few hundred guests. The website has two jobs. It has to sell tickets for the next event, and it has to hold years of photographs and videos from the events before it.

Chris Byrne, the founder, also needed to run it himself, adding a new night with its photos and video without calling anyone for help.

What I built

  1. Event pages that sell the night

    Each event gets its own page with the flyer shown whole, the venue, directions, and a ticket link. Upcoming nights lead the homepage.

  2. An archive kept at full quality

    All 43 photo albums are hosted on the new site. Pages load fast web copies, and each photograph can be downloaded one at a time at the photographer’s original quality. Whole albums can’t be downloaded, by design.

  3. Video in high resolution

    Recap videos from past nights play on the site from the highest-quality files available, up to 4K.

  4. The mailing list stayed in one place

    The signup form on the new site feeds the email list Chris already used, so nothing had to be moved or kept in two places.

Keeping it up to date

Chris adds a night from a single upload page. He enters the name, date, and venue, drops in the photographer’s folder and the video, and the site builds the album, the web copies, and the download links.

Events and albums can be saved as drafts. The homepage, sponsors, partners, and team are all editable from the same login.
