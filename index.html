Update it yourself, without learning a website builder

Custom Websites and Full Builds come with an editor made for your site. It has presets for the things you add most, so a new event, book, concert, album, or post is a short form to fill in, not a blank page to design.

The fields match your content. An event asks for a date, a venue, and a ticket link. A book asks for a publication date, a description, and where to buy it. The site handles the layout, so every new page looks like it belongs.

You can save a draft before it goes live, and earlier versions of the site can be restored. If you’d rather send me the change, that works too.

A few things I pay attention to

Speed

In Portent’s 2022 research, B2B websites loading in one second had three times the conversion rate of sites loading in five seconds.

That doesn’t mean every faster website will triple its business, but it is a good reason to take loading performance seriously.

Portent, 2022

Accessibility

WebAIM’s 2026 analysis found automatically detectable accessibility failures on 95.9% of one million homepages.

I pay attention to readable text, properly labeled forms, keyboard navigation, and other basics that make websites easier to use.

WebAIM, 2026

Search

Each page gets its own title and description, written so people searching can tell what they’ll find.

When a site moves, I keep existing page addresses where I can and redirect the rest.

What does a website cost?

A simple starter website costs $300 to $500. Most custom websites cost between $500 and $1,200. Larger projects with archives, event systems, or other features start at $1,200 and are priced by scope.
