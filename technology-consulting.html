Technology help for small organizations

I help small businesses, nonprofits, and offices get more out of the technology they already use.

That might mean sorting out email accounts and shared files, connecting different services, setting up an AI assistant, or figuring out why a particular system has become difficult to manage.

I can review what you have, recommend changes, and help put them into practice.

Using AI at work

AI tools can be useful for drafting, research, organizing information, and routine administrative tasks. They can also introduce mistakes, privacy concerns, and extra work when they’re set up badly.

I help organizations decide where these tools might be useful, what information they should have access to, and how their work should be checked.

Sometimes the best recommendation is not to automate something at all.

AI Setup

Choosing and configuring an assistant for a particular person or organization.

Workflow Review

Looking at repeated tasks and determining whether AI or ordinary automation could make them easier.

Supervised Automation

Setting up a limited process with clear permissions, human review, and a way to stop or change it.

Staff Training

Working through actual tasks with staff, including where the tools perform well and where they need checking.

AI Guidelines

Helping draft straightforward policies on approved tools, confidential information, and human oversight.

What the research says about AI

Small organizations are adopting AI, but many are still figuring out how to use it effectively.

Nearly 70% say they need more training

Thryv’s 2026 survey found that nearly seven in ten small-business owners felt they needed additional AI skills or training.

Rather than introducing a collection of new tools, I prefer to begin with the tasks and services a client already uses.

Thryv, 2026

Workflow changes matter

McKinsey’s 2026 State of AI survey found that nearly three-quarters of its highest-performing AI adopters had fundamentally redesigned workflows, compared with approximately one-quarter of other respondents. The survey includes large organizations and is not research exclusively about small businesses.

For my clients, the practical question is usually simpler. What task is taking too much time, and is AI actually the right way to improve it?

McKinsey, 2026

Examples of the work

Moving client forms to a new service

When the form service behind my clients’ contact forms announced it was shutting down in 2026, I set up a replacement, Web3Forms, with a separate key for each client tied to their own email, so their messages never pass through my accounts.

An upload page for an events company

DC Social Collective adds each event’s photos and video from one page. The original files are stored at full quality, and the site builds the album and download links on its own.

New writing that appears on its own

Roxane Salonen publishes on Substack, and her newest posts appear on her website automatically, so she only has to post once.

Moving a domain without moving the owner

For Frank La Rocca, I moved his website to new hosting and sorted out conflicting settings from an old provider, while his domain stayed registered in his own name.
